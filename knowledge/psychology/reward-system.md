# Reward System

> [Behavioral Science index](00-index.md). Applied in [docs/16](../../docs/16-behavioral-design.md).
> **The most abusable principle here — held to the strictest ethical guardrail.**

## Concept
Behavior that is rewarded repeats. The brain's dopamine system responds strongly to **anticipation**
and **variable** rewards (unpredictable payoffs drive more repetition than fixed ones) — the same
mechanism slot machines and infinite feeds exploit.

## Why it matters
Rewards can reinforce healthy logging — or be twisted into compulsive engagement. LifeLedger uses
rewards **only** to reinforce behaviors that serve the user's own goal, and deliberately avoids the
addictive-design playbook ([why-no-ads.md](../../decisions/why-no-ads.md): attention is not the product).

## Evidence
- Dopamine and reward prediction / variable-ratio reinforcement. **B** `[verify]`
- Variable rewards in persuasive tech (Hooked model, Eyal) — cited as a pattern to use *carefully*. **C** `[verify]`

## How LifeLedger applies it (gently)
- **Predictable, honest rewards:** progress rings fill, goals met are acknowledged, insights arrive.
- **Meaningful reward = understanding:** the real payoff is "you learned something about your body,"
  not points.
- **Mild, optional celebration** of streaks/milestones — never escalating, never manipulative.

## Anti-patterns to avoid (explicit refusals)
- Variable-reward loops engineered to maximize app-opens.
- Infinite scroll, FOMO mechanics, or notification bait.
- Rewarding *engagement* over *health outcomes*.
