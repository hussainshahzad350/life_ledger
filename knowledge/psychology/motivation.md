# Motivation

> [Behavioral Science index](00-index.md). Applied in [docs/16](../../docs/16-behavioral-design.md).

## Concept
Motivation splits into **intrinsic** (doing something because it's satisfying/meaningful) and
**extrinsic** (external rewards/pressure). **Self-Determination Theory** (Deci & Ryan) holds that
durable motivation grows from three needs: **autonomy**, **competence**, and **relatedness**.

## Why it matters
Motivation is unreliable and fluctuates — designing *around* it (habits, low friction) beats relying
*on* it. But the app can still nurture intrinsic motivation so users stay for understanding, not points.

## Evidence
- Self-Determination Theory — Deci & Ryan. **B** `[verify]`
- Intrinsic motivation's durability vs. extrinsic rewards (overjustification effect). **B/C** `[verify]`

## How LifeLedger applies it
- **Autonomy:** user owns their data and **overrides any goal** ([docs/08 F2](../../docs/08-feature-specs.md)); no forced targets.
- **Competence:** small wins, clear progress, and insights that make the user feel they're *learning
  their body* ([docs/01 mission](../../docs/01-vision.md)).
- **Relatedness:** (future, careful) optional family mode; never social comparison/competition.
- Lead with **intrinsic** framing ("understand yourself"), not extrinsic pressure.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- Manipulative extrinsic hooks (loss-framed streaks, badges-for-badges' sake) that crowd out the real
  reward of understanding.
- Comparison/leaderboards — explicitly a non-goal ([docs/01](../../docs/01-vision.md)).
