# Habit Formation

> [Behavioral Science index](00-index.md). Applied in [docs/16 — Behavioral Design](../../docs/16-behavioral-design.md).

## Concept
Habits form through a loop: **Cue → Routine → Reward** (popularized by Charles Duhigg). Repeated
enough in a stable context, a behavior becomes automatic, requiring little conscious effort or
motivation. Habits are context-triggered, which is why *when/where* matters as much as *what*.

## Why it matters
LifeLedger's whole value depends on **consistent logging**. If logging relies on willpower, it fails.
The goal is to make logging an automatic habit anchored to existing cues (after a meal, on waking).

## Evidence
- Habit-loop model — Duhigg, *The Power of Habit*. **B** `[verify]`
- Context-dependent automaticity of habits — behavioral psychology literature. **B/C** `[verify]`
- (Popular claim of "21 days to a habit" is a **myth**; formation time varies widely.) **C**

## How LifeLedger applies it
- **Cue:** opt-in reminders anchored to natural moments (post-meal), plus the dashboard as a visible cue.
- **Routine:** the **< 10s log** ([docs/05 §5.2](../../docs/05-uiux-system.md)) — friction is the enemy of routine.
- **Reward:** immediate visual progress (rings filling), streaks, and a supportive insight
  → [reward-system.md](reward-system.md), [streak-psychology.md](streak-psychology.md).

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- Guilt cues ("you failed again") — these poison the loop and drive uninstalls.
- Demanding perfection; missing a day must never reset the user's sense of progress.
