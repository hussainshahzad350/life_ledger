# Atomic Habits — Concepts

> [Behavioral Science index](00-index.md). Applied in [docs/16](../../docs/16-behavioral-design.md).
> Concepts attributed to **James Clear, *Atomic Habits*** (described conceptually; `[verify]` citations).

## Concept
Small (atomic) improvements compound. Clear's **Four Laws** for building a good habit:
1. **Make it obvious** (cue) 2. **Make it attractive** (craving)
3. **Make it easy** (response) 4. **Make it satisfying** (reward)
Plus **identity-based habits**: focus on becoming "the kind of person who tracks their health," not
just hitting a number.

## Why it matters
This is a practical checklist LifeLedger can apply directly to logging behavior, and the
identity angle aligns with the mission of *understanding oneself* rather than chasing metrics.

## Evidence
- *Atomic Habits* — James Clear (popular synthesis of behavioral science). **B/C** `[verify]`
- Underlying cue/craving/response/reward loop overlaps with [habit-formation.md](habit-formation.md). **B**

## How LifeLedger applies it
- **Obvious:** dashboard as a persistent cue; anchored reminders.
- **Attractive:** clean, calm UI; supportive insights that feel rewarding to read.
- **Easy:** the < 10s log; defaults; recents/favorites ([decision-fatigue.md](decision-fatigue.md)).
- **Satisfying:** immediate progress rings, gentle streaks, "why?" insight payoff.
- **Identity:** framing ("you're becoming someone who understands their body"), never guilt.

## Anti-patterns to avoid
- "Attractive" tipping into manipulative/addictive design (variable rewards abused) — see
  [reward-system.md](reward-system.md).
- Optimizing "satisfying" for app-opens instead of user benefit.
