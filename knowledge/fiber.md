# Fiber

> [Knowledge Base](00-index.md) · Nutrition.

## Definition
Fiber is a carbohydrate the body cannot fully digest. **Soluble** fiber (oats, legumes, fruit) slows
digestion and can help cholesterol/blood sugar; **insoluble** fiber (whole grains, vegetable skins)
adds bulk and aids regularity.

## Benefits
- Improves **digestive regularity** and stool bulk. *(A)* → [digestion.md](digestion.md)
- Increases **satiety**, supporting appetite control. *(B)*
- Feeds beneficial **gut bacteria** (fermentable fibers → short-chain fatty acids). *(B/C)* → [gut_health.md](gut_health.md)
- Associated with lower risk of heart disease and improved blood-sugar control. *(B)*

## Deficiency
Low fiber intake is common and associated with constipation and less favorable metabolic markers.
A frequent, practical target to improve. *(B.)*

## Upper Limits
No formal UL; very rapid increases (especially without adequate water) can cause bloating/gas.
Guidance: increase gradually and hydrate. *(B.)*

## Common Misconceptions
- *"Fiber supplements equal whole-food fiber."* Whole foods bring a mix of fibers + nutrients;
  supplements are partial. *(B)*
- *"More fiber is always better."* Very high intake without water can worsen symptoms. *(C)*
