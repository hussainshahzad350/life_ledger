# Sleep

> [Knowledge Base](00-index.md) · Health.

## Definition
Sleep is the body's essential recovery process, governing physical repair, memory consolidation,
hormone regulation (including appetite hormones), and mood. Most adults need roughly **7–9 hours**
per night.

## Benefits
- Recovery, immune function, and memory consolidation. *(A)*
- Regulates **appetite hormones** (leptin/ghrelin) — poor sleep is linked to increased hunger,
  connecting to the mission question *"why am I always hungry?"*. *(B)*
- Supports **mood** and stress resilience. *(B)*

## Deficiency
Short or poor sleep is associated with fatigue, low mood, impaired concentration, increased appetite,
and, over time, metabolic risk. LifeLedger surfaces the user's *own* sleep↔mood/energy patterns
rather than making clinical claims. *(A/B general; D at the individual pattern level.)*

## Upper Limits
Consistently very long sleep (>9–10h) can correlate with underlying issues, but the app does not
interpret this medically — it only reflects the data. *(C.)*

## Common Misconceptions
- *"You can fully catch up on lost sleep at weekends."* Partial recovery only; consistency matters. *(B)*
- *"Everyone needs exactly 8 hours."* Needs vary; 7–9h is a range, not a fixed number. *(B)*
- *"More sleep is always better."* Quality and consistency matter, not just duration. *(C)*

## Evidence Level
Core role & appetite link: **A/B**. Individual mood correlations: **D** (personal pattern only).
